Core Objects

The paper separates the problem into a free-carrier electronic screening part and an undoped polar-phonon part. The doped carriers are added in a rigid-band picture; local-field effects are neglected for the long-wavelength macroscopic response.

Step 1: Free-Carrier RPA

The supplemental material defines the bare doped-carrier response as

δχ0(q,ω) = (1/Nk) ∑mnk [δfm,k+q - δfn,k] |<um,k+q|un,k>|2 / [εm,k+q - εn,k + ω + i0+].

The interacting carrier response follows from the scalar RPA screened Coulomb kernel

Uq = 4πe2 / [q2 ε∞qhat V],   ε∞qhat = qhat · ε∞ · qhat.
εel-1(q,ω) = 1 + Uqδχ(q,ω) = [1 - Uqδχ0(q,ω)]-1.

The paper evaluates the wavefunction overlap using Wannier interpolation: diagonalize H^W(k) and H^W(k+q) in the same Wannier basis and use the overlap of their eigenvectors. For very small q, an intraband identity-overlap approximation can be used as a controlled diagnostic, but the paper-level route is the Wannier-eigenvector overlap.

Step 2: Plasmon-Pole Compression

Rather than carrying the full frequency dependence of δχ into every phonon calculation, the paper fits the RPA inverse dielectric function to a damped plasmon pole:

εel-1(q,ω) - 1 = Uqδχ(q,ω) = Ωq02 / [(ω + iγq0)2 - ωq02].

The fitted parameters are the plasmon strength Ωq0, plasmon frequency ωq0, and linewidth γq0. In the computational details, GaAs uses a 200x200x200 k grid for the plasmon-pole parameters, an energy window of 0-0.3 eV around the CBM, and η=3 meV smearing in the RPA denominator.

Step 3: Long-Range Polar Vertex

For the hybridization mechanism relevant to Fig. 1, the supplement keeps the dominant dipolar long-range electron-phonon vertex:

gqνlr = i [4πe2/(Vq)] ∑κ [qhat · Z*κ · eκν(q) / sqrt(Mκ)] / ε∞qhat.

The eigen-displacements are normalized as ∑κα e*κμ,α eκν,α = δμν. The short-range electron-phonon vertex is not needed for the long-wavelength hybrid energies in Fig. 1; it matters for more general electron self-energy calculations.

Step 4A: Direct Dyson Route

The nonadiabatic phonon self-energy induced by the doped carriers is

Πμν(q,ω) = [gqμlr]* δχ(q,ω) gqνlr.

With the bare phonon Green function

D0μν(q,ω) = δμν / [(ω + iγqν)2 - ωqν2],

the interacting phonon response is obtained from

D-1μν(q,ω) = [D0]-1μν(q,ω) - Πμν(q,ω).

The phonon spectral function used for neutron-like response plots is

A(q,ω) = -(2ω/π) ∑ν Im Dνν(q,ω).

Step 4B: Equivalent Hybrid-Mode Route

The plasmon-pole representation allows the frequency-dependent Dyson problem to be rewritten as a frequency-independent plasmon-phonon problem. Define the long-range electron-plasmon vertex and coupling amplitude:

gq0lr = sqrt(Ωq02 Uq),   cqν = gq0lr gqνlr / Uq = Ωq0 gqνlr / sqrt(Uq).

Then the self-energy can be written as a plasmon propagator mediating phonon-phonon coupling:

Πμν(q,ω) = cqμ* D000(q,ω) cqν,   D000 = 1/[(ω+iγq0)2 - ωq02].

For zero linewidths, the hybrid energies are obtained by diagonalizing the (Nph+1)x(Nph+1) plasmon-phonon dynamical matrix:

Ctildeq = [[ωq02, cq1, cq2, ...], [cq1*, ωq12, 0, ...], [cq2*, 0, ωq22, ...], ...].
Ctildeq Vtildeqβ = omega_tildeqβ2 Vtildeqβ.

The eigenvalues give the plasmon-phonon hybrid energies; the eigenvectors give plasmon and phonon weights. With finite linewidths, the paper solves the corresponding damped-oscillator problem, yielding complex eigenvalues whose real parts are hybrid energies and imaginary parts are linewidths.

Total Inverse Dielectric Function

The electron-loss response is not just the phonon spectral function. The supplement defines it from the long-range effective electron-electron interaction W00^pl-ph:

εpl-ph-1(q,ω) = [q2V/(4πe2)] W00pl-ph(q,ω).

In the hybrid basis, the finite-linewidth form is

W00pl-ph(q,ω) = Uq + ∑β [gtildeqβlr,L (gtildeqβlr,R)* / (ω - omega_tildeqβ + i gamma_tildeqβ) + (ω → -ω)*].

For infinitesimal linewidths, the supplement gives the compact squared-frequency form

εpl-ph-1(q,ω) = (1/ε∞qhat) [1 + (1/Uq) ∑β 2 omega_tildeqβ |gtildeqβlr|2 / {(ω+i0+)2 - omega_tildeqβ2}].

Here the hybrid long-range vertex is the plasmon/phonon vertex projected onto each hybrid eigenmode:

gtildeqβlr = ∑α=0Nph gqαlr Vtildeqαβ.

The measured EELS-like quantity in the paper is typically -Im εpl-ph-1(q,ω). Only modes with nonzero projected long-range charge vertex contribute strongly to this dielectric loss.

Practical Calculation Recipe

  1. Choose carrier density, temperature, q magnitude, and q direction. The paper's calculations use T=300 K; GaAs Fig. 1 uses |q|=8e-4 Angstrom^-1 along [110].
  2. From the undoped electronic structure, solve the chemical potential for the doped carrier density and compute δf.
  3. Evaluate δχ0(q,ω) on a frequency grid using Wannier-interpolated energies and overlaps.
  4. Build εel^{-1} from the RPA formula and fit Ωq0, ωq0, and γq0 to the plasmon-pole model.
  5. Obtain undoped phonon frequencies/eigenvectors, Born effective charges, ε∞, masses, and cell volume from DFPT.
  6. Compute gqν^lr and c qν. Keep only the IR-active longitudinal mode for a two-mode Fig. 1-style GaAs plot, or keep all modes for a full spectral function.
  7. For branch energies, diagonalize the hybrid dynamical matrix. For spectral functions, either reconstruct D from hybrid modes or solve Dyson on the frequency grid.
  8. For dielectric/EELS response, compute W00^pl-ph and εpl-ph^{-1}, then plot -Im εpl-ph^{-1}.

Implementation Notes

For direct Fig. 1 energies in GaAs, the short-range EPW electron-phonon vertex is unnecessary because the avoided crossing is controlled by the long-range polar charge vertex. EPW/Wannier data are still useful for the electronic RPA input and for general self-energy calculations.

The identity-overlap approximation is a diagnostic, not the default paper route. It is most defensible for very small q and a single slowly varying conduction band. The direct reproduction path should record whether each RPA table used wannier_eigenvector or identity_delta overlap.
